2. NON-NEGOTIABLE DOMAIN INVARIANTS
- Board Definition is topology only. Assignment is a separate exact map from slot to component kind, Owned Instance, Definition ID/version and source revision.
- Definition and Owned Instance are distinct. Multiple instances of one Definition retain independent identity/growth. Master/Slave are role slots, not intrinsic Workshop Definition identities.
- Jar is a discrete identity-bearing resource with shape, internal liquid and Mark. Liquid is either Jar-internal or free-liquid staging; these are separate accounts.
- Jar polarity is derived: empty=Neutral, 酒=Cathode, 蜜=Anode.
- Workshop Ban expresses focus polarity: Ban 酒 => Anode focus; Ban 蜜 => Cathode focus. Neutral Jar is operable by either focus; opposite-polarity Jar is not. No Ban preserves legacy dual-polarity compatibility; both bans permit Neutral only.
- Actual Jar operations (MATCH, POUR_IN, POUR_OUT, RESHAPE) check operating Workshop polarity. Jar possession and physical handoff do not.
- Master-to-Slave Jar transfer changes ownership only. Preserve exact Jar ID, shape, internal liquid, derived polarity and Mark. Never require the receiver to operate a Jar merely to receive it. Never add CanOperateJar/polarity gate to handoff.
- Free-liquid staging has its own Ban/capacity admission. PourOut's Jar operation and subsequent staging settlement are distinct transitions; accepted quantity transfers to staging, rejected/overflow quantity is explicitly discarded.
- Master staging Ban blocks that free liquid from transfer to Slave. B-class Slave starts without its own Jar/free-liquid inventory as order inputs; it receives permitted resources from Master.
- Order-produced free liquid first attempts current Workshop staging; rejection/overflow discards it. No hidden banned-liquid staging account.
- MATCH marks exact Jar; handoff preserves Mark. Clear only this order's participating Jar marks at terminal success/failure/cancel; unrelated marks survive.
- Unknown/unimplemented rules and unsupported scopes fail closed as Undetermined/Rejected. Budget exhaustion is Undetermined, never proof of Infeasible.

3. B-CLASS ROLE ASSIGNMENT MODEL
- Both Master and Slave candidate profiles participate in demand assignment. The enumerator uses Slave-first ordering for compatibility, then tries Master alternatives when needed.
- Each requirement is fulfilled exactly once by one role. A B-class plan must contain at least one concrete Slave demand operation; a pure Jar-only handoff is not a replacement for the B-class Slave-use event.
- Master demand operations execute before handoff; Slave demand operations execute after handoff.
- A Master-completed requirement Jar may still be handed off as B-class output without a second Slave operation.
- Master preparation may fund Master operations and may cross the liquid handoff boundary for Slave operations only when source and destination staging rules permit it.
- Slave preparation only funds Slave-side needs.
- B-class plans require at least one concrete Slave demand operation; pure Jar-only handoff is not a substitute for the B-class Slave-use event.
- Old precomputedSolutions is intentionally ignored until its cache key fingerprints the complete Master+Slave profile set. A stale Slave-only solution set can omit Master branches.
- Current enumerator is bounded, not a universal circuit compiler. Verify mixed-role schedules, operation order, resource replay, materialization, manifest and execution.
